\section*{Thesis Outcomes and Contributions}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\protect\numberline{}Thesis Outcomes and Contributions}

This thesis delivers the three contributions announced in the abstract, evaluated against the design science framework of \textcite{Hevner2004} and the outcome evaluation guidance of \textcite{Peffers2007}. The first is a \textbf{requirements specification} for an application security data platform, motivated and grounded in \autoref{ch:analysis}. The specification text was published on the documentation site and attached to this thesis as \texttt{appsec-mvp-docs.zip} instead of placed in an appendix because of its length. The second is a \textbf{reusable and extensible framework} (\autoref{ch:framework}) covering reference architecture, data model, and the medallion based pipeline~\parencite{Strengholt2025} from source records to consumption. The third is a \textbf{reference implementation} (\autoref{ch:implementation}) on Databricks against nine selected data sources, attached to this thesis as \texttt{appsec-mvp.zip}.

The methodology maps concretely to the work. The framework is the design artifact (Hevner: design as an artifact; Peffers: design and development), motivated by the application security data consolidation problem in \autoref{ch:analysis} (Hevner: problem relevance; Peffers: identify the problem and define objectives). It is instantiated in \autoref{ch:implementation} (Peffers: demonstration), and evaluated through the traceability matrix and the \gls{ai} instantiability assessment in \autoref{sec:ai-eval} (Hevner: design evaluation and research contributions; Peffers: evaluation). Rigor comes from tests bound to requirement identifiers and from the iteration summary (Hevner: research rigor and design as a search process). Communication runs through the thesis and the documentation site (Hevner: communication of research; Peffers: communication). Two items are partial: the \gls{ai} evaluation criterion was settled after the first reviewer cycles, not declared in advance, and design alternatives are recorded only at the level of the iteration summary.

The framework is \textbf{reusable} in the concrete sense that \autoref{ch:implementation} introduced no new connector level design decisions beyond which sources to onboard. It is \textbf{extensible} in the sense that onboarding a new source reduces to the five step procedure of \autoref{sec:impl-methodology}: category resolution, module instantiation, declarative mapping, layered testing, and skill chain pass with reviewer supervision. ServiceNow on Lakeflow Connect and GitHub on PyGitHub are concrete examples of the same contract supporting different integration patterns.

\section*{Evaluation of AI Instantiability}
\label{sec:ai-eval}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\protect\numberline{}Evaluation of AI Instantiability}

\autoref{ch:framework} aimed the framework outputs at an \gls{ai} coding agent. The original target was fully autonomous generation from the agent \href{https://vkraus.github.io/appsec-mvp/connectors/scm/skills/}{skill catalog}. The \gls{mvp} was produced by invocations of that catalog under reviewer subagent supervision (Subagent-Driven Development).

\subsection*{Acceptance criterion}

A correction counts as a \textbf{framework gap} when extending the specification (schema pattern, connector contract from \autoref{sec:connector-contract}, or declarative artifact) would have produced the right output on first pass and the extension would generalize. Corrections tracing to \textbf{source specific issues} or \textbf{environment setup} (setup, authentication, infrastructure) do not count against the framework.

\subsection*{Empirical outcome}

The four-skill chain ran across all nine source specifications, producing connector modules for each source and validation evidence. \autoref{sec:iteration-summary} enumerates the defects the reviewer cycle caught: a layering boundary (framework gap), schema drift between \gls{sql} \gls{ddl} and PySpark types (framework gap), a secret scope mismatch (environment setup), worktree pollution (environment setup), a cron schedule collision (source specific issue), and IRSA OIDC trust policy gaps (environment setup). Two of six classify as framework gaps. Evidence lives in the \href{https://vkraus.github.io/appsec-mvp/}{documentation site}.

\subsection*{Claim defended}

The integration layer of the framework, that is every per source connector that adapts a security tool to the lakehouse contract, is \gls{ai} \textbf{instantiable} under skill catalog invocation. The reference implementation generated connector modules for nine sources by the four skill chain with reviewer subagent cycles catching defects. \autoref{sec:impl-results} separates generated artifacts, live source exercise, and Spark wrapper completeness. The platform layer the connectors run against and the analytics layer they feed are deliberately hand written: the platform is the singleton contract the skills depend on by name, and the analytics layer is the specific evidence the thesis presents. The contract, mapping, and module layout reduce new connector work to a five step procedure that converges with bounded supervision. It is not yet \gls{ai} \textbf{autonomous}: every connector still required reviewer cycles, and the schema drift gap plus the open items below remain. The empirical evidence above was produced by a single operator (the author) acting as both implementer and reviewer of the evaluation, without inter rater reliability or an independent grader. Closing the open items, refining the skills until invocations are self sustaining, and replicating the evaluation under a controlled study with multiple operators would measure the distance from instantiable to autonomous. That measurement is the subject of Future Work.

\section*{Limitations}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\protect\numberline{}Limitations}

The \gls{mvp} is a working reference rather than a production ready integration. It is \textbf{platform specific}: it runs on Databricks over \gls{aws} object storage, the only combination exercised end to end. \textbf{Live tenant exercise} is partial: of the nine connectors, ServiceNow, GitHub, SonarQube, Semgrep, and \gls{owasp} \gls{zap} have been exercised against live source systems during the construction of this thesis. The remaining four (GitLab, Dependency-Track, TruffleHog, \gls{aws} \gls{waf}) pass their unit tests against fixtures but have not been exercised against a live tenant. The connector contract in \autoref{sec:connector-framework} is written to be cloud and platform agnostic in principle, but it has not been ported to Snowflake, Microsoft Fabric, or an on premises platform, so the portability claim rests on contract design alone, with no second demonstrated implementation.

\textbf{Reproducibility} requires a Databricks workspace with Unity Catalog, an \gls{aws} account with an S3 bucket and federated credentials, a Lakeflow Connect entitlement, a ServiceNow developer instance, and a GitHub Personal Access Token (\gls{pat}). The Databricks Asset Bundle deploys every workspace resource the bundle format covers natively. The post deploy shell script \inlinecode{src/platform/scripts/bootstrap.sh} creates the secret scope container, Unity Catalog storage credential, and Unity Catalog external location. Per connector Terraform modules under \inlinecode{src/connectors/<source>/runtime/} provision source side cloud infrastructure. The archives attached to this thesis, \inlinecode{appsec-mvp.zip} and \inlinecode{appsec-mvp-docs.zip}, cannot reproduce the build without these entitlements.

Several \textbf{open implementation items} remain, but they are narrower than the pre-redesign framework gaps summarized in \autoref{sec:iteration-summary}. Connector population of \inlinecode{silver.repositories} is partial: GitHub writes the standard narrow repository fields, while wider target fields and GitLab parity remain future work. \inlinecode{silver.app\_repo\_mapping} is not empty. The platform layer populates it through a name and application code linker over \inlinecode{silver.applications} and \inlinecode{silver.repositories}. Parallel CMDB relationship paths through \inlinecode{cmdb\_rel\_ci} and explicit repository link columns remain pending. Several Spark connector wrappers currently return empty Silver DataFrames with target schemas while their mapping helpers at row level are tested. SonarQube and \gls{owasp} \gls{zap} have non-empty Spark transforms. Some notebook entry wrappers generated by skills remain placeholders. Dependency-Track live dltool execution and the \gls{aws} \gls{waf} boto3 sampled request fallback remain deferred, while the WAF log stream path is the preferred design. Inherited error handling sharp edges remain in \inlinecode{bootstrap.sh} and the ServiceNow Terraform runtime, both flagged for hardening.

The analytics and serving layer is implemented as evidence for the thesis rather than a production service. It includes five Gold datasets, one Gold view, two Online Table resources, suppression rules, and a Databricks App. The tests are backed by fixtures, mocked, or marked for Spark/live execution as appropriate. The thesis does not claim production deployment, measured serving latency, or trained \gls{ml} models.

The \textbf{variation} of the selected sources is biased toward \gls{rest} \gls{json} \glspl{api} with token authentication (five of the nine: GitHub, GitLab, ServiceNow, SonarQube, Dependency-Track). \gls{aws} \gls{waf} consumes a log stream from Kinesis Data Firehose to S3 with SigV4 service credentials rather than a bearer token, \gls{owasp} \gls{zap} follows an artifact pattern, and the two file based sources (Semgrep, TruffleHog) deliver via S3 instead of an interactive \gls{api}. Source categories not demonstrated include SOAP, gRPC, syslog, Kafka event streams, binary telemetry, and push only webhook native sources. The contract is written to accommodate these, but evidence is absent on a category by category basis.

\section*{Future Work}
\label{sec:future-work}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\protect\numberline{}Future Work}

\textbf{Closing the remaining framework gap.} The layering boundary gap was closed by the structural redesign. The schema drift gap remains open. A single source of truth for silver schemas resolving the drift between \gls{sql} \gls{ddl} and PySpark \inlinecode{StructType} definitions is the precondition for any stronger \gls{ai} autonomy claim.

\textbf{Completing the declarative mapping applicator and Spark wrapper wiring.} \autoref{sec:transformation-patterns} treats \inlinecode{mapping.yml} as the authoritative specification consumed by a generic applicator, but the \gls{mvp} applies mappings through Python helpers and Spark code for each source. Completing the applicator and replacing the remaining wrappers with non-empty Spark transforms would make adding a source closer to a configuration edit than a code edit.

\textbf{Realizing the remaining prescribed silver tables.} The framework prescribes 15 silver tables (\autoref{sec:entity-model}). The \gls{mvp} realizes the core subset \inlinecode{applications}, \inlinecode{repositories}, \inlinecode{findings}, \inlinecode{finding\_location}, \inlinecode{hwm}, \inlinecode{app\_repo\_mapping}, and \inlinecode{suppression\_rules}. Future iterations should add the remaining entity tables (\inlinecode{teams}, \inlinecode{commits}, \inlinecode{pull\_requests}, \inlinecode{pipeline\_runs}, \inlinecode{dependencies}, \inlinecode{branch\_policies}), the reference tables (\inlinecode{vulnerabilities} from the \gls{nvd}, \inlinecode{epss\_scores}, \inlinecode{kev\_entries}), and the remaining relationship table \inlinecode{finding\_cve\_mapping}. Cross tool overlap is currently computed in Gold rather than stored in a separate \inlinecode{dedup\_links} table. Adding a persisted dedup relationship remains a future extension.

\textbf{Finishing job orchestration and bootstrap hardening.} Some notebook wrappers generated by skills still need full job orchestration, and the remaining Spark wrappers need implementation. The bootstrap script and ServiceNow runtime need the hardening pass named in Limitations.

\textbf{Live analytics evidence and downstream triage.} The rule based Gold layer and Databricks App now exist, so the next step is to run them against live multi-source data, measure serving latency, and connect them to downstream triage workflows. Large Language Model assisted triage and correlation over the gold tables is a natural extension and would exercise the data model of \autoref{sec:data-model} in the direction it was designed for.
