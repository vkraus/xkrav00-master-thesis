\chapter{Analysis and Requirements}
\label{ch:analysis}

This chapter surveys the literature and analyzes the functional domains relevant to building an application security data platform. Each section covers one domain, combining theoretical foundations with practical tool and \gls{api} analysis. The focus is on reviewing existing knowledge, observing source system interfaces, data models, and integration patterns, not on prescribing a solution. Technology choices for the target platform are deferred to \autoref{ch:framework} and \autoref{ch:reference-implementation}.

\autoref{sec:app-inventory} establishes the business context underlying all security findings. \autoref{sec:sw-dev-literature} and \autoref{sec:appsec-literature} examine the software development and security tooling landscapes as data sources. \autoref{sec:data-literature} covers the data engineering patterns that underpin the framework's design. Finally, \autoref{sec:related-work} surveys existing solutions and identifies the gap this thesis addresses.

\section{Application Inventory}
\label{sec:app-inventory}

The application inventory is the foundational data source for any application security data platform. It establishes the business context for all security findings. Without a reliable catalog of business applications and their mapping to technical assets, a vulnerability discovered in a repository or a running service cannot be attributed to an owner, assessed for business impact, or prioritized against organizational risk criteria.

%%% 1.1.1 %%%

\subsection{Application Portfolio Management}

Enterprise organizations maintain catalogs of their business applications with metadata such as application name, description, ownership information, lifecycle status, and relationships to other assets~\cite{AXELOS2019}.

These registries also capture security-relevant attributes. Information security is commonly assessed along three dimensions known as the CIA triad: confidentiality (preventing unauthorized disclosure), integrity (preventing unauthorized modification), and availability (ensuring authorized access)~\cite{Stallings2024}. Business impact assessments evaluate all three CIA dimensions to produce criticality ratings, commonly structured as tiers: Tier~1 for mission-critical applications, Tier~2 for important supporting systems, and Tier~3 for non-critical internal tools. Separately, regulatory scope flags identify which compliance frameworks (e.g., PCI~DSS, GDPR, SOX) apply to a given application.

The most important attribute from a data integration perspective is the mapping between business applications and their technical assets. A single business application may span multiple source code repositories, microservices, infrastructure components, and deployment environments. This mapping serves as the glue connecting technical security findings to business context. When the framework ingests a vulnerability from a \gls{sast} tool associated with a specific repository, the application inventory mapping determines which business application is affected, who owns it, and how critical it is. This enables the risk-aware prioritization that stakeholders require.

%%% 1.1.2 %%%

\subsection{Configuration Management Databases}

Organizations typically maintain their application inventories in \glspl{cmdb} such as ServiceNow, in custom-built internal catalogs, or in a combination of both. A \gls{cmdb} organizes all managed assets as \glspl{ci}, each with attributes describing its type, status, and ownership~\cite{AXELOS2019}.

ServiceNow is the leading \gls{cmdb} platform in enterprise environments, holding over 44\% of the global \gls{itsm} market share~\cite{Gartner2024ITSM}, and serves as the reference platform for this thesis. Its \gls{cmdb} arranges \glspl{ci} in a class hierarchy rooted at the \texttt{cmdb\_ci} base table. Each subclass inherits base attributes (name, \texttt{sys\_id}, operational status, environment) and adds domain-specific fields. For application inventory purposes, the most relevant class is \texttt{cmdb\_ci\_appl} (Application), which extends the base \gls{ci} with attributes such as version, business unit, used-for classification, and references to the owning support group~\cite{ServiceNowCMDB2024}.

Beyond the application record itself, the \gls{cmdb} captures relationships between \glspl{ci} through a dedicated relationship table (\texttt{cmdb\_rel\_ci}). These relationships model dependencies such as ``runs on'' (application to server), ``depends on'' (application to database), and ``hosted on'' (application to cloud service). For the framework, the most valuable relationships are those linking business applications to technical assets that correspond to entities in the security data: repository names, deployment targets, and infrastructure components. ServiceNow also allows organizations to define custom attributes and relationship types, so the specific fields available for ingestion may vary between deployments.

\subsection{Integration Options}

Integrating \gls{cmdb} data into an external data platform requires choosing between two strategies: pulling data from ServiceNow via its \glspl{api}, or pushing data from a custom ServiceNow application to an external target.

\subsubsection{ServiceNow APIs}

ServiceNow exposes two complementary \gls{rest} \glspl{api} for \gls{cmdb} data extraction. The Table \gls{api} provides generic CRUD operations against any ServiceNow table through the endpoint \texttt{/api/now/table/\{tableName\}}~\cite{ServiceNowTableAPI2024}. Responses are \gls{json}-formatted, with support for server-side filtering (\texttt{sysparm\_query}), field selection (\texttt{sysparm\_fields}), and pagination (\texttt{sysparm\_limit} combined with \texttt{sysparm\_offset}, with a default maximum of 10\,000~records per request). The \texttt{sysparm\_display\_value} parameter resolves reference fields to human-readable names instead of internal \texttt{sys\_id} values.

The \gls{cmdb} Instance \gls{api} (\texttt{/now/cmdb/instance/\{className\}}) provides a \gls{cmdb}-aware alternative that understands the \gls{ci} class hierarchy~\cite{ServiceNowCMDB2024}. It can retrieve a \gls{ci} with its relationships in a single call, reducing the number of \gls{api} calls needed to reconstruct the application-to-asset mapping. However, it is more constrained in its query capabilities than the Table \gls{api}.

Both \glspl{api} require authentication, typically through Basic Authentication with a service account granted the \texttt{rest\_service} or \texttt{cmdb\_read} role, or through OAuth~2.0 for more security-conscious deployments. Rate limits vary by instance configuration and are governed by platform transaction quotas rather than per-endpoint limits. For incremental data extraction, the \texttt{sys\_updated\_on} timestamp field present on all records serves as a reliable high-water mark, allowing consumers to query only records modified since the last extraction.

Any pull-based integration must handle authentication, pagination, rate limiting, and incremental state management. Several integration libraries and platform-native connectors abstract these concerns; the specific tooling choice depends on the target data platform and is discussed in \autoref{sec:servicenow-impl}.

\subsubsection{ServiceNow Application}

An alternative to pulling data is to push it from the source. ServiceNow supports building scoped applications that run on the platform itself. A custom application can use Outbound REST Messages to send \gls{cmdb} data to an external target (e.g., a cloud storage bucket or a \gls{rest} endpoint) on a schedule defined through Scheduled Jobs or Flow Designer. The application can also expose a Scripted \gls{rest} \gls{api}~\cite{ServiceNowScriptedREST2024}, a custom endpoint that shapes the data exactly as the consumer needs it, pre-joining application records with their relationships and custom attributes in a single response.

The advantage is full access to ServiceNow's internal scripting capabilities, relationship traversal, and business logic without external \gls{api} call overhead. The disadvantage is that development and maintenance happen inside the ServiceNow platform, requiring ServiceNow development expertise and a separate deployment lifecycle. Changes to the \gls{cmdb} schema or extraction logic must be coordinated across two platforms. This approach is best suited for organizations with mature ServiceNow development teams that prefer to own the data export logic on the source side.

\subsubsection{Integration Challenges}

The application inventory presents the most significant integration challenge among all data sources because it is the least standardized. Security testing tools converge around common \gls{api} patterns and output formats, but each organization structures its application inventory differently: the hierarchy of applications, the granularity of asset mapping, and the attributes captured vary substantially. This makes the application inventory connector the hardest to generalize and the one most likely to require organization-specific customization.

Data quality in \glspl{cmdb} is a persistent concern. Application records may be incomplete (missing ownership or criticality assignments), outdated (reflecting decommissioned applications still listed as active), or inconsistently maintained across business units. Any integration must account for these quality issues through validation rules and default values that surface problems without blocking the data pipeline.

Despite these difficulties, the application inventory is indispensable. Without the business context it provides, security findings lack the organizational meaning needed for effective prioritization and governance.
